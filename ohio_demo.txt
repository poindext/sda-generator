For a credible statewide Ohio demonstration, I recommend 500,000 synthetic patients covering all 88 counties, with 24 months of history.

That is large enough to demonstrate county-level differences, demographic disparities, chronic-disease cohorts, and drill-downs without creating unnecessary infrastructure overhead.

Recommended dataset
Data domain	Suggested volume
Unique patients	500,000
Counties	All 88
Encounters/ADT events	3–5 million
Diagnoses	4–8 million
Laboratory results	5–10 million
Vital-sign observations	8–15 million
Facilities	150–250
Providers	5,000–10,000
Historical period	24 months
New simulated activity	10,000–25,000 clinical events/day

These are approximate ranges; the records should overlap around the same patients rather than representing separate populations.

Suggested disease cohorts

For 500,000 patients:

Cohort	Approximate patients
Diabetes	55,000–70,000
Hypertension	120,000–160,000
Asthma	40,000–55,000
Cardiovascular disease	35,000–50,000
Pregnancies during the period	8,000–12,000
Reportable-disease cases	3,000–8,000
Patients with multiple chronic conditions	60,000–90,000

Within the diabetes cohort, I would intentionally generate:

15–20% with an A1c above 9%
20–25% with no A1c received by OHIP in the preceding six months
Meaningful differences by county, age, race/ethnicity, Medicaid status, and rurality
A few counties showing improving trends
One or two counties showing a recent increase requiring investigation
Geographic distribution

Allocate patients approximately according to county population, but establish a minimum of 750–1,000 patients per county. Strict population-weighted generation would leave Ohio’s smallest counties with too few records for useful demographic breakdowns.

The dashboard can still calculate weighted statewide measures so deliberately oversampling rural counties does not distort the statewide results.

Reportable-disease demonstration

Rare diseases can disappear in statistically realistic synthetic data. Deliberately seed a small outbreak scenario:

A normal baseline of 1–3 cases per week
A rise to 10–15 positive results in one county
Related ED visits appearing several days before confirmed laboratory results
Spread into one neighboring county
Demographic and facility-level clustering
A visible reporting and confirmation timeline

That provides a much stronger demonstration than randomly generated cases.

My practical recommendation

Build the initial demo with:

500,000 patients, 24 months of history, approximately 20–30 million clinical facts, and 30 days of simulated near-real-time activity.

If storage or generation time becomes an issue, 250,000 patients is still adequate. I would not go below 100,000 for something presented as a statewide Ohio dashboard, because county and demographic intersections will begin looking artificial or will require extensive suppression.

The synthetic data should also include deliberately incomplete participating-source coverage. That will let us demonstrate a critical OHIP feature: distinguishing a true population trend from a change caused by a hospital or laboratory feed becoming unavailable.
